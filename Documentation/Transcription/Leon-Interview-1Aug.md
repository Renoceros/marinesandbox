**[00:22:07] Leon**: I think it's not so much about restoration or corals per se; it's about getting into the ocean. Because a lot of times, you cannot care about what you don't see. Part of the reason why the ocean is so destroyed is because for the longest time, we could not see below the surface. Unless you're a diver or a snorkeler, you don't actually see it for yourself. Awareness has to come first: realizing that the ocean is a magical, real experience that you want to return to. From there, awareness leads to conservation: "It's beautiful, I want to protect it." If you go a few times and see it's not looking so good, you naturally ask, "Why is this not good? Is there something we are doing?" That leads to a conservation mindset. Once you get to that step, you start to think critically. After that, you start to not just care, but actually do something about it. The end stage would be restoration, which is what I'm doing, where you really do more for it. Get people in the ocean. ### Part 3: Post-Experience Connection & Personalization

**[00:25:23] Bobo**: For the people who come to your place, how there is a whole array of engagement from super engaged to not so engaged. For those who are super engaged, is there anything they can do or interact with after they are done experiencing and planting the corals, to continue to explore and engage with the conservation efforts?

**[00:26:06] Leon**: One of the things we try to do is the bamboo tags. You saw the generic bamboo, but for donors, we personalize it with their name. Each structure underwater has a tag, and one of my staff goes down and takes photos of it every month. We take the three photos you saw: the bamboo tag, the side view, and the top view. Every month, we take these three photos for every donor, upload them to our cloud system, and send them an email with the link.

That's a small way of keeping them engaged. They can see over a few months how their coral has grown, died, or progressed. Of course, we cannot monitor it forever because after a while, the bamboo disintegrates and gets covered over by coral growth. But we know the general area where we planted them, so if people come back, we can show them.

I've been thinking about community development: how do we engage all these donors who donated structures over the long term? Currently, we send a couple of emails, but to be honest, your generation doesn't even read email, so a lot of it gets lost. If there was a way to keep them engaged—uploading more videos, photos, and updates of what's going on—that would be nice. If this is something I personally did, I want to see how it progressed. If someone else did it, it's just a random reef and I don't really care as much. Personalization is what allows that connection to stay. And if there was a community, they could engage, come back, or buy more structures. I've been thinking about building something like that, but I haven't figured out how yet.
